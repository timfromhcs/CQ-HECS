# OMNIWAVE: research report (v3.5.0, experimental)

Status: **experimental research engine.** It is correct by construction of its
evidence (SAT with a verified model, UNSAT only with a DRAT proof accepted by
`cqhecs.drat`), but it is slower than the built-in DPLL on most of the measured
instances and much slower than CaDiCaL. Nothing in this report is a speedup
claim or a complexity claim.

Evidence files (generated by `scripts/omniwave_benchmark.py`, fixed seeds):

- `research/v5/evidence/omniwave_benchmark.json`: engine comparison (§5)
- `research/v5/evidence/omniwave_ablation.json`: H1/H2 ablation (§6)

## 2. Sources

The sources named in the add-on were looked up before this work and are cited
for the ideas they contain. None of their code is used.

| Source | Idea taken | Used as |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Nadel, Shalmon, Strichman: Gpap (arXiv 2610.28805) | GPU BCP over many independent partial assignments of one formula; Gpap-S runs many decide-and-propagate trajectories at once | the batched BCP layout (one formula, many bit-packed fronts). Gpap-S is a sampler; OMNIWAVE uses the batched-propagation idea only, inside an exhaustive search |
| Schreiber, Sanders: MallobSat, JAIR 2024 (doi:10.1613/jair.1.15827) | clause sharing between parallel solvers | the shared nogood pool (short clauses only) |
| Michaelson et al.: Producing Proofs of Unsatisfiability with Distributed Clause-Sharing SAT Solvers, JAR 2025 (doi:10.1007/s10817-025-09725-w) | shared clauses need proof provenance; a complete proof is reconstructed and checked independently | the rule "log the lemma before sharing the nogood", and one DRAT proof for all workers |
| Heule et al.: Cube-and-Conquer (github.com/marijnheule/CnC) | split the problem into cubes and solve the cubes | fronts are cubes; the cube tree gives the proof structure |
| Soos, Meel: BIRD, AAAI 2019 (doi:10.1609/aaai.v33i01.33011592) | CNF-XOR solving, XOR hashing for approximate counting | the exact XOR view and the parity-hash cells, with the rule that a hash cell never proves UNSAT |
| OpenAI ETH artifact, `quantitative-reduction.tex` at `4eaee87` | the claimed `2^(O(n/log n))`-type reduction | treated as a **hypothesis** only (§7); see `COMPLEXITY_CLAIM_AUDIT.md` |

Novelty caveat: every mechanism above is known. Multi-order cube search with
clause sharing, batched GPU propagation, CNF-XOR views and separator dynamic
programming all exist in the literature. OMNIWAVE combines them under one proof
discipline in a small, testable code base. It does not claim a new algorithm.

## 3. Correctness argument

1. **Splits are exhaustive.** Every open front with an unassigned variable `x`
   is replaced by the two fronts `cube + x` and `cube + not-x`. Their models
   partition the models of the parent. (Mutant 50 drops one branch; it is killed.)
2. **Leaf lemmas are RUP.** A front is refuted when unit propagation on
   `F + nogoods + cube` reaches a conflict. Every nogood used was logged earlier
   in the proof, so "not cube" follows by reverse unit propagation from the
   clauses that precede it in the proof.
3. **Nogoods are shared only after logging.** The clause "not cube" enters the
   shared pool in the same locked step that appends it to the proof. (Mutant 52
   shares a stronger, unlogged clause; it is killed.)
4. **Parent lemmas are RUP.** When both children of a cube `C` are refuted, the
   lemmas "not (C + x)" and "not (C + not-x)" are in the proof, and "not C"
   follows from them by unit propagation.
5. **The root lemma is the empty clause.** UNSAT is reported only when
   `cqhecs.drat.check_drat` returns `valid is True` for the formula and the whole
   proof. An undecided check (budget) gives UNKNOWN. (Mutant 53 accepts an
   undecided check; it is killed.)
6. **Dedup is exact.** Fronts are compared by their sorted literal tuples.
   (Mutant 51 dedups by an 8-bit hash; it is killed.)
7. **GPU results are checked.** The Vulkan pass must equal the CPU reference bit
   for bit; a difference raises. A wrong GPU result could only change the search
   order, because the DRAT check is independent of it.
8. **Coverage is exact.** A refuted front at depth `d` adds `2^-d`; when both
   children are refuted their share is replaced by the parent's. Coverage 1.0
   means the root is refuted.

## 4. Representations: exhaustive oracles

`tests/test_omniwave.py` checks every view against brute force on small
formulas: XOR detection and GF(2) solution counts, the quadratic encoding
(equal model count, degree ≤ 2), rank factorization (`M = UV`, rank, bilinear
form preserved), tensor counting (equals `count_models`, raises `TableTooLarge`
instead of approximating), separator counting, parity-hash buckets (they
partition the solutions), and the batched BCP status words (scalar, batch and
Vulkan identical).

- The GF(2) view refutes every parity instance in under 1 ms; DPLL needs 20 s and
  more for the 4x4 torus. The tensor count matches #SAT on the banded instances
  and is 9-310x faster there.

## 6. Hypotheses

### H1: several waves with shared nogoods reduce the work of the search

Measured as fronts processed (a machine-independent work count) for five
configurations on six UNSAT and two SAT instances in
`omniwave_ablation.json`:

| instance | verdict | 1 wave, no sharing | 1 wave, sharing <= 8 | 4 waves, no sharing | 4 waves, sharing <= 8 | 4 waves, 2 workers, sharing <= 8 |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `benchmarks/sat/pigeonhole_6_5.cnf` | UNSAT | 945 | 945 | 777 | 777 | 777 |
| `tseitin_torus(3x3,seed=3009)` | UNSAT | 2047 | 2047 | 2047 | 2047 | 2047 |
| `random:3sat:n=30:m=128:seed=1510` | SAT | 46 | 46 | 46 | 46 | 46 |
| `random:3sat:n=30:m=128:seed=1511` | UNSAT | 255 | 255 | 241 | 241 | 241 |
| `random:3sat:n=30:m=128:seed=1512` | UNSAT | 183 | 183 | 135 | 135 | 135 |
| `random:3sat:n=40:m=170:seed=1680` | SAT | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 |
| `random:3sat:n=40:m=170:seed=1681` | UNSAT | 299 | 299 | 131 | 131 | 131 |
| `random:3sat:n=40:m=170:seed=1682` | UNSAT | 137 | 137 | 217 | 217 | 217 |

Findings:

- **Nogood sharing had no measurable effect** in any configuration. With one
  wave this is a theorem: all fronts follow one variable order, so a refuted
  cube is only a subset of its own descendants, which are never created. With
  four waves the orders differ between subtrees, but no shared clause pruned a
  front in these instances.
- **Several waves changed the work in both directions**: fewer fronts on four
  of six UNSAT instances (up to 56 % fewer), more on one (+58 %), no change on
  the parity formula. That is a property of the different branching orders, not
  of parallelism.
- Two workers processed exactly the same fronts as one worker here; the work is
  split, not reduced, and the threads share the GIL.

Verdict: **H1 not supported** in the form "sharing reduces work". The useful
part is the per-subtree branching order, and its effect depends on the instance.

### H2: exact algebraic signatures compress the state space

Two parts were measured:

- **XOR structure (parity family).** The GF(2) view detects every parity
  constraint of the Tseitin formulas exactly and finds the system inconsistent
  (0 solutions) in milliseconds, where every clause-level engine needs
  exponential resolution work. This is the known CNF-XOR effect (BIRD); it is
  not new. OMNIWAVE does not yet feed the GF(2) verdict into its proof, so the
  search still pays the clause-level cost (and a GF(2) inconsistency is not
  reported as UNSAT without a DRAT proof).
- **Separator signatures (banded family).** Left-side partial states that agree
  on a separator of width 5 merge into at most 32 exact signatures:

  | instance | consistent left states | distinct signatures | separator count = #SAT |
  | :--- | ---: | ---: | :--- |
  | `banded_3sat(n=24,m=60,w=5,seed=5000)` | 148 | 4 | yes (1464) |
  | `banded_3sat(n=24,m=60,w=5,seed=5001)` | 952 | 9 | yes (5692) |
  | `banded_3sat(n=24,m=60,w=5,seed=5002)` | 1020 | 14 | yes (7200) |
  | `banded_3sat(n=24,m=60,w=5,seed=5003)` | 240 | 11 | yes (1512) |
  | `banded_3sat(n=24,m=60,w=5,seed=5004)` | 302 | 7 | yes (4436) |

  The separator count equals the exact #SAT count in every case. This is the
  classical treewidth dynamic program; compression is bounded by `2^width` and
  disappears on formulas with wide separators (random 3-SAT).

Verdict: **H2 supported only where it was already known to hold** (XOR
structure, small separators). No evidence of compression on random 3-SAT.

## 7. The `2^(O(n/log n))` question

The add-on asked to treat a sub-exponential bound as a hypothesis. Nothing
measured here bears on it: the instances are small, the work counts of every
engine (including OMNIWAVE) grow exponentially across the parity and random
families, and a finite benchmark cannot confirm or refute an asymptotic bound.
The ETH status itself is recorded in `COMPLEXITY_CLAIM_AUDIT.md` (claimed
refuted, Lean-formalized, not peer-reviewed, not re-run here). P vs NP stays
open.

## 8. Limits

- Python worker threads share the GIL: more workers do not run faster.
- The batched CPU mode evaluates every clause for every front on every pass (no
  watched literals). It is a reference for the GPU kernel, not a fast path, and
  it is slower than scalar mode in every measured family.
- The Vulkan mode moves the whole formula and all fronts per pass and checks
  each pass on the CPU, so it is slower than the CPU modes. It shows that the
  kernel is correct on real hardware; it is not GPU acceleration.
- No learning beyond the shared cube nogoods (no CDCL), no restarts, no
  preprocessing. DPLL beats OMNIWAVE on most instances; CaDiCaL beats both.
- The front queue is bounded (`max_queue`, `max_fronts`); a run that hits a
  bound reports UNKNOWN with its exact coverage.
- `cqhecs.drat` is independent of the engine but not formally verified (no
  `cake_lpr` here, `BLOCKERS.md` B-02).

## 9. Non-claims

- No P = NP claim, no ETH refutation, no universal speedup.
- No GPU acceleration claim.
- The benchmark timings are one run on one machine.
